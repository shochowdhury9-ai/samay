घटना-क्रम Event log

    प्रलय The dissolutions

    नित्य प्रलय

    Nitya pralaya

    Constant dissolution — continuous change and ending of individual beings.

    नैमित्तिक प्रलय

    Naimittika pralaya

    At the end of every kalpa, when Brahmā's day ends and the three worlds dissolve.

    प्राकृतिक प्रलय

    Prākṛtika pralaya

    Elemental dissolution at the end of Brahmā's life of two parārdhas.

    आत्यन्तिक प्रलय

    Ātyantika pralaya

    Final liberation — when an individual soul is freed from rebirth.

    महाप्रलय

    Mahāpralaya

    Not a larger unit after parārdha, but the event that ends Brahmā's full life; generally identified with prākṛtika pralaya. Only the imperishable Brahman remains — and, in the cyclical view, a new Brahmā and new kalpas arise.

    About the numbers
    • 1 ahorātra = 24 h. A human year (saṃvatsara) = 360 days. 1 divine day-night = 1 human year; 1 divine year = 360 human years.
    • Durations of paramāṇu → lava are derived from the nimeṣa value of the chosen chain using the Bhāgavata ratios (16,200 paramāṇu = 1 nimeṣa). With the Bhāgavata nimeṣa (≈ 0.4267 s) one paramāṇu is ≈ 26.3 µs. (The source table lists ≈ 2.63 µs for paramāṇu, which is ten times smaller than its own ratios imply — here the ratios are kept consistent.)
    • Brahmā's day (kalpa) = 14 manvantaras × 71 caturyugas (994 caturyugas) + 15 sandhyā of 4,800 divya-varṣa each (= 6 caturyuga-equivalents) = 1,000 caturyugas. Yuga and manvantara counters therefore run only during Brahmā's day; the equal night is pralaya.
    • Counting uses exact integer arithmetic (BigInt, picoseconds), so counts stay accurate even at 10,000,000× speed. Very large numbers are shown as mantissa ×10n.
    • At 10,000,000× it would still take about 1015 real seconds to reach mahāpralaya, so use “Skip ahead” to witness the big events.

    महाप्रलय

    Mahāpralaya

    Brahmā's full life of two parārdhas is complete. The manifested universe is withdrawn; only the imperishable Brahman, the cause of all causes, remains. In the cyclical view, a new Brahmā and a new sequence of kalpas arise.
